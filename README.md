# PETRA — Petri Evolution & Trait Response Arena

> Working title: **Project Petra**

PETRA is a browser-based interactive microbial-evolution sandbox designed to **feel like a game while behaving like a scientific model**.

The core visual is a living Petri dish. Users alter environmental pressures — nutrients, temperature, antimicrobial exposure, competing populations, and eventually bacteriophages — and watch populations expand, collapse, diversify, and evolve. Outcomes are generated by a seeded stochastic simulation, not pre-rendered videos.

## Flagship scenario

The first scientifically anchored build models a spatially structured *E. coli* population growing on a limiting resource while exposed to ciprofloxacin.

The MVP combines:

- a 2D nutrient field with diffusion and local consumption;
- local resource-limited growth;
- concentration-dependent ciprofloxacin pharmacodynamics;
- spontaneous mutation during reproduction;
- curated resistant genotype/phenotype states with literature-backed MIC and fitness data;
- spatial selection and lineage competition;
- deterministic random seeds for replay;
- a lineage tree, population plots and a source/provenance panel.

## Scientific commitments

PETRA will not claim to be a digital twin of a real culture. It is a mechanistic educational simulator with an explicit claim ledger.

- Mutation occurs during reproduction; drug exposure selects variants rather than “telling” cells how to mutate.
- Resistance is not free by default: genotype-specific fitness is represented where data exist.
- Spatial nutrient/drug gradients matter.
- Shared-resource competition can emerge without arbitrary “attack” coefficients.
- Every validated parameter has a source, units, experimental context and transfer caveat.
- Visible bacteria are a rendering layer; they are not a one-particle-per-cell simulation.
- PETRA is not a clinical dosing or treatment tool.

## Design rule

**Mechanistic engine first; spectacle second.** Animations may interpolate the simulation state, but they never decide the biology.
