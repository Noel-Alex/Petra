# Collateral sensitivity and cross-resistance

Resistance evolution can change susceptibility to other antibiotics.

Primary educational anchor:
- Imamovic & Sommer (2013), *Science Translational Medicine*, DOI `10.1126/scitranslmed.3006609`: evolved *E. coli* against 23 drugs, mapped collateral sensitivity/resistance, and demonstrated compatible cycling examples.

Additional modern mapping work confirms that both cross-resistance and collateral sensitivity are widespread but mechanism/genotype dependent.

## Petra implications
- Store drug-to-drug effects as measured genotype/trajectory-specific data.
- Do not create a universal rule that “resistance to drug A makes drug B stronger.”
- Repeated evolution can be non-repeatable; a drug sequence that works for one resistance mechanism may not for another.
- A multi-drug challenge should display uncertainty and the measured network behind each edge.

This is promising for a future strategy-game mode, but it remains separate from the first ciprofloxacin scenario.
