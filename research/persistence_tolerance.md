# Persistence and tolerance — evidence note

## Core distinction
Antibiotic **resistance** is a heritable change in susceptibility. **Persistence** is a reversible phenotypic state in which a subpopulation survives antibiotic exposure without stably inheriting resistance.

Primary anchor: Balaban et al. (2004), *Science*, DOI `10.1126/science.1099390`.

Key result relevant to Petra: cells regrown from persisters remain sensitive, and single-cell experiments linked persistence to pre-existing heterogeneity/phenotypic switching.

A follow-up modeling anchor is Kussell et al. (2005), *Genetics*, DOI `10.1534/genetics.104.035352`, framing reversible switching as a bet-hedging strategy in changing environments.

## Petra rule
Model persistence as phenotype compartments inside one genotype:
- growing/susceptible state;
- slow/dormant tolerant state;
- reversible switching rates.

The drug may strongly change death rates between states, but a survivor does not become genetically resistant unless a separate mutation occurs.

## Calibration status
Mechanism: strong.
Universal switching rates: **not available**. Rates depend on strain, growth condition, stress, and operational definition. Initial presets must either use a named experiment or clearly label calibrated/educational switching parameters.
